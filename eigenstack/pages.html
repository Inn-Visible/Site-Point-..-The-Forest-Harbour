stack 4 · core textbooks — where logic meets heat & copper
covered: 22f·22b · 23f · 24f · 25f · backs 23–25: null
  • 1. control systems (the spine): “if you read 1 serious book, make it this one.” Franklin et al — gold standard · teaches why systems behave, not just math. Ogata — classical & state-space intuition. “these explain why systems oscillate, drift, saturate, or go unstable.”
  • 2. practical electronics: The Art of Electronics — Horowitz & Hill · the bible for sensors · interfaces · power · signal integrity · reads like a senior engineer talking to you. “not a control book per se — but it teaches how signals survive reality.”
  • 3. power + motors: Hughes & Drury, Electric Motors & Drives · Mohan, Power Electronics (switch-mode supplies · drives · inverters · why power stages misbehave). “this is where ‘logic meets heat & copper’.”
  • 4. sensors & measurement: Doebelin, Measurement Systems — sensors · noise · error · calibration · why readings lie to you. “if control is steering, sensors are the windshield.”
  • engagement paths (to stay sane): Brian Douglas · Brunton Bootcamp · Real Engineering · EEVblog · GreatScott!
  • stitch-together (non-academic): Douglas vids → Franklin & Powell (read slowly, not linearly) → AoE (parallel reference) → motors/power (once feedback clicks). NB// “you’re not training to be a ‘chip-surgeon’. you’re training to look at a system & say: ‘ah. that loop is fighting itself.’ ‘that sensor is lying.’ ‘that interface is injecting chaos.’ — that’s system literacy.”
