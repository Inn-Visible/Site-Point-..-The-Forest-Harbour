stack 6 · infrastructure — page 27, front & back [transcribed]
  • “if you want a real education in this, read these in order.”
  • 1. Seeing Like a State — James C. Scott
    foundational. it explains: why systems flatten reality · why infrastructure simplifies humans · why legibility = power.
    margin: “this book will change how you see everything.”
  • 2. The Power Broker — Robert Caro
    not about a man. about how infrastructure becomes destiny. you’ll see: how control hides behind “public good” · how permanence beats popularity · how power accumulates quietly.
  • 3. Infrastructure: A Field Guide — Brian Larkin
    more academic, but explains: how infrastructure shapes behavior · why invisible systems dominate · how culture follows pipes & wires.
  • 4. The Utopia of Rules — David Graeber
    explains bureaucracy as a power structure. very relevant to modern life.
  • 5. Platform Capitalism — Nick Srnicek
    explains how modern infrastructure = data + coordination. (this explains Uber, Amazon, Google, etc.)
stack 7 · infrastructure cont’d — pages 28–29 [transcribed]
  • 1. “The Art of Strategy” — Avinash Dixit & Barry Nalebuff
    this one hits close. it teaches: how people act in systems · how leverage emerges · how positioning beats effort · why indirect moves win.
    margin: “it’s not moral. it’s not academic. it’s practical logic.”
  • 2. “The 33 Strategies of War” — Robert Greene
    not the 48 laws. this one is different. it’s about: psychological terrain · structural advantage · knowing when not to engage · creating asymmetry.
    margin: “less slimy. more strategic.”
  • 3. “Debt: The First 5000 Years” — David Graeber
    this one hits deep. explaining: why money is power · how debt controls people · why economics aren’t fair · how obligation creates hierarchy.
    margin: “this will probably resonate w/ you hard.”
  • 4. “The Box” — Marc Levinson
    this one surprises people. it’s about shipping containers. why it matters: explains how logistics reshaped the world · shows how infrastructure quietly controls everything · makes clear how boring innovations create massive leverage.
    margin: “if you understand containers, you understand globalization.”
  • 5. fiction that teaches power better than nonfiction
    some novels teach this better than textbooks.
    Dune — power, control, systems, ecology · The Dispossessed — political structure · Neuromancer — systems, corporations, leverage · The Wire (TV) — probably the best depiction of real power ever made.
    margin: “these show power in motion. (lens)”
